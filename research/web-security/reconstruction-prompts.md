# Reconstructing the investigation

These prompts support a later author revision. They are not presented as completed findings, and answering them is not required to preserve the existing history.

1. What do I remember first seeing: an application behavior, an error, a page, a configuration issue, or something else?
2. What access did I have, and what scope was I working within?
3. What did I test, in what order, and what changed my understanding?
4. Which weakness do I remember, and what evidence distinguishes that explanation from alternatives?
5. Which archived files did I obtain directly, and which relate to separate investigations or later collecting?
6. Did I document an outcome, contact an operator, stop after establishing the finding, or take another step? Include only what I actually remember or can substantiate.
7. Which details are certain, approximate, or no longer recoverable?

Write from memory first without adding actual personal records. Then compare the account with any surviving contemporary notes. Mark disagreements and uncertain details explicitly.

An appropriate opening is: “Around 2007–2008, I investigated …” followed only by details I can support. A precise date, vulnerability label, affected organization, or successful outcome should not be supplied just to make the narrative feel complete.
